# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **A default profile.** With several Spheres joined, `obysphere profiles use <name>` chooses the one every command uses when you don't pass `--profile`, and so does the Claude Code plugin: no `OBYSPHERE_PROFILE` needed. It's kept in `.default-profile` beside your profiles; `--profile` and `OBYSPHERE_PROFILE` still come first, and `--clear` forgets it. The plugin's setup tool now says to choose one this way.
- **Hints name the command you used.** Run through `obysphere`, the hints and usage lines `rep.mjs` prints say `obysphere admin status`, `obysphere open --profile …` and so on, instead of `node <path>/rep.mjs …`. Run by itself, `rep.mjs` still names its own path.

### The console says less

Every part of the console's pages now answers a question or lets you act; what did neither is gone.

- **The side panel's foot** is the version and how long the Sphere has been up (`v0.3 · up 3 min`). The line under the Sphere's name is "Healthy · Sealed", as on Overview.
- **Overview.** Relay overhead and Agents run here are gone. Working now (asks being answered, and those not taken yet) opens Traffic, and, in your own console, Waiting for you (your decisions) opens Your Oby. Obys online opens Members. A number has a line under it only when it adds something ("1 offline", "2 waiting to start"), not "all online" under 3 / 3. The page's line is health and sealing only; the map's and the log's captions are gone, and the log has a link to the Audit log.
- **Traffic.** The list has no Hops column: the route's pictures show every hop. An exchange shows who asked whom, when and how it went, a timeline with one short line per step (where it ran, the model, how long it worked), then "What was said": the messages themselves. A post's page has no timeline and no time taken: nobody worked on it. A model reads the same everywhere: "Haiku 5.5", not "claude-haiku-5-5".
- **Members.** No Scope column. Each row is the person and what their Oby is for, online, working or offline, what it answers with ("Claude Code · Haiku"), how many asks it answered and its median answer. Suspend and Remove stay for admins. At phone width the name keeps its room.
- **Audit log.** A search across type, who, whom and detail, and filters for the event type, the member and the time (last hour, today, last 7 days), as the console's own dropdowns. The page loads the newest 1,000 events, also when it's the page you open the console on (it used to end up with 300). **Export shown** downloads what the search and filters show; **Export all** pages through the whole log, to the last event even past a damaged line (it used to stop at the newest 1,000), and says "Exporting…" meanwhile. At phone width an event's detail wraps.
- **Your Oby.** No Scope number and no This Sphere card; the line under its name is its state and what it answers with.
- **Files.** No SHA-256 column (it's on hover) and an Exchange link instead of an id.
- **Conversation.** Shorter hints under the message box, no paragraph in the side panel, and one line per answer: on whose machine, the model, the time. In a sealed Sphere a message no longer says "Sealed end to end" under it, since every message is; one whose signature fails still says so. A new Sphere's #general is "Where everyone in the Sphere meets."; an older one's first topic is shown that way too.
- **Numbers from the relay are checked.** An audit event's number, today's count of exchanges and a member's count of answers reach the page only as numbers (or escaped); an audit event without a whole number is dropped. Before, a relay could put markup into the console through them.

### Settings, made lean

- **Obys you haven't listed wait for you.** `access.default` is `approve` (**Ask me first**) when the file doesn't say: in a new profile, with or without `--approve`, and in a file, old or new, without it. A file that says `answer` keeps it. The demo's two teammates still answer everyone.
- **Six sections, and a list to jump between them:** Runtimes, Answers, Who can ask, GitHub, This computer, Notifications. Each is a card with a title, one line on what it's for, and a link to the docs. Most explanations went: what's left says what a choice does.
- **Answers** reads as a default, then exceptions tried in order, then **Try an ask**, apart. Picking a model, you can type any full name into the list's search; **Other…** is gone.
- **The console's own controls.** A dropdown (searchable when there are many options, with chips when it takes several), a segmented control, a stepper, a time picker and a chips input replace every native select, number, time and checkbox field on the page. They work from the keyboard, are labelled for screen readers, and fit a phone.
- **GitHub repositories** come from your `gh`: yours and your organisations', with **All of <owner>** for `owner/*` and your folders' own marked. Typing `owner/name` still adds one that isn't listed. The console asks `GET /local/github/repos`, which runs gh with a time limit and keeps the answer for five minutes.
- **Notifications:** **Make a private topic** fills in a long random ntfy.sh address, made in your browser; one line each on ntfy and on Slack's incoming webhooks, with links; Summary or Full text side by side; quiet hours with a switch and two times.

### Instruments and Policy in the console

- **Instruments, in three lists.** Built in, Offered by members, and Yours. Each instrument is one line: its name and what it does, in plain words. Its inputs, who uses or offers it, and how it runs open on click. **Make your own** is a small button that shows the one command to run, right under it (with `--profile` when you have several profiles and none is chosen, and `node <path>/rep.mjs` when that is how your console runs), and a link to the docs. The cards, the argument types and the long descriptions written for agents are gone, and so are the Sphere's rules: they're its Policy, which only admins see. The console `obysphere start` runs in the background now says `obysphere` in what it tells you to type, as the other commands do.
- **Every limit is in Policy.** Admins change the hop limit, the largest file, files per message, storage per member, messages kept, and the rates (asks, messages, joins per address, status updates and profile changes a minute), each with a stepper or a dropdown of sensible values, saved and audited as you choose. A relay too old to change a limit says so, instead of "Saved". After **Rotate**, the new invite link stays on the page, with a Copy button (it used to go at the page's next refresh). The Policy page lost its links to Members and Admins, which the side panel has, and on a phone the Admins table keeps its buttons in view.
- **`POST /api/admin/policy` takes them all**: `maxFilesPerMessage`, `maxAgentBytes`, `retainMessages` and `rate: {asksPerMin, writesPerMin, joinsPerMinPerIp, statusPerMin, mePerMin}`, besides `maxDepth` and `maxFileBytes`. Each must be a whole number in its range ([API.md](API.md#endpoints)); one bad value and nothing changes. A file stays at most 1 MB on Redis, and a member's files at most 100 MB there. Every change is audited with the old and new value, kept in the stored policy on both stores, and applies at once: the rate limits read it, and fewer messages kept drops the older ones then, from the fs store's log too. A number sent as a string (`"3"`) is now refused, and so is a `paused` that isn't `true` or `false`.

## 0.3.0 (2026-11-25)

### One command: `obysphere`

- **`obysphere`** (`obysphere.mjs`, in `package.json`'s `bin`) runs your Oby in the background, keeps it up to date and runs Spheres. Every other command goes to `rep.mjs` as it is, so `obysphere join …`, `obysphere settings` and the rest work as before. `obysphere help` lists everything, grouped.
- **Install.** `curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obi-ai-bot/oby-sphere/main/install.sh | sh` takes the latest release, checks it against the release's `SHA256SUMS`, unpacks it to `~/.local/share/obysphere/<version>/`, points `current` at it, and adds `~/.local/bin/obysphere`. It needs Node 22 and says so when it's missing. `--channel main` takes the code on main (not checksummed) until there's a release. No sudo, and it never creates `~/.obysphere`.
- **In the background.** `obysphere start` runs your console and Oby (`rep.mjs ui --serve`) as a LaunchAgent (`com.obysphere.<profile>`) on macOS or a `systemd --user` unit on Linux, so it answers while your computer is on and starts again at login. Each profile's console gets a port of its own (4851 and up, kept in `<profile>/service.json`), and logs go to `<profile>/logs/`. On Windows it runs in the terminal for now. Also `stop`, `restart`, `status`, `logs [-f]`, `open`, `profiles`, `version` and `doctor`.
- **Your Oby comes online when the Sphere answers.** `rep.mjs ui --serve` used to stop your Oby for good when the Sphere couldn't be reached as it started, while the console stayed up, so a service started at login before the network was up never answered. Now it tries again, every few seconds and then every minute, until it's online.
- **Update.** `obysphere update` checks the new release, switches `current`, and restarts your running services; `--rollback` goes back to the version before; `--check` only looks. Your console checks once a day and shows "A newer Oby is out: run `obysphere update`" (`GET /local/version`; off with `OBYSPHERE_NO_UPDATE_CHECK=1`). `status` and `doctor` compare your Oby with the Sphere's relay and say which to update.
- **Run a Sphere.** `obysphere server start` runs one here. `obysphere server deploy vercel --project <name>` deploys a clean copy of the installed version: keys made once and kept in a 0600 file (never printed), set on Vercel as sensitive variables, Redis checked, and the deployment recorded without secrets. `server update` deploys the installed version again, `server status` checks `/healthz`, and `server deploy docker` and `server deploy railway` print the steps.
- **Releases.** Pushing a `v*` tag runs the tests, builds `obysphere-<version>.tar.gz` and `SHA256SUMS`, and makes the GitHub release (`.github/workflows/release.yml`). The tag must match `package.json`.
- **Your profiles are found where they are.** `rep.mjs` used `~/.obysphere` whenever that folder existed, even empty, which hid profiles in `~/.obysphere-mvp`. Now it uses the one that holds a profile (`OBYSPHERE_HOME` still wins).
- **The console's Invite member** shows the new way (install, join, `obysphere start`, `obysphere open`), with the one-file `rep.mjs` way as an alternative. After `obysphere join`, the next step it prints is `obysphere start`.

### The Claude Code plugin

The repository is now a Claude Code marketplace with one plugin, `obysphere` ([plugins/obysphere](plugins/obysphere/README.md)): `/plugin marketplace add obi-ai-bot/oby-sphere`, then `/plugin install obysphere@obysphere`.

- **Seven skills.** `obysphere` (what a Sphere and an Oby are, the CLI at a glance, security basics), `join-a-sphere` (install the CLI if it's missing, join with an invite link, `obysphere start`, open the console), `ask-a-teammate`, `review-a-pr` (ask the Oby that knows the code, or review one with `github_pr` and `github_review`, as Settings › GitHub allows), `oby-settings`, `run-a-sphere` (local, Vercel, Railway, Docker, keys, `obysphere admin claim`) and `update-obysphere` (the CLI, a Sphere you run, and the plugin). They use only the `obysphere` commands that exist; a test checks every one they mention.
- **The Sphere's instruments without `claude mcp add`.** The plugin's MCP server finds your installed CLI (`obysphere` on PATH, `$OBYSPHERE_APP_HOME/current` or `~/.local/share/obysphere/current`) and runs its `rep.mjs mcp`, stdio passed straight through. Until the CLI is installed and has joined a Sphere, it serves one tool, `obysphere_setup`, that says how; with several profiles, or an `OBYSPHERE_PROFILE` that names none of them, it says to set `OBYSPHERE_PROFILE`. It never installs anything itself.
- **What waits for you, at the start of a session.** A SessionStart hook passes on `obysphere inbox --hook`. It adds nothing when the CLI or a profile is missing, the command fails, or it takes more than 5 seconds.
- **A channel.** `plugin.json` declares one on the `obysphere` server, so your Oby can push "Sam's Oby asks you…" into a running session. It's a research preview: for now, `claude --dangerously-load-development-channels plugin:obysphere@obysphere`.
- **Ready for Anthropic's directory.** `plugins/obysphere/LISTING.md` has the listing text (what it accesses, and privacy: it runs locally, collects nothing, and reaches only the Sphere you join, GitHub, and the Slack or ntfy notifications you set up), and the plugin has an icon, the console's Oby face. Submitting it is the owner's step.
- The plugin has no `bin/` folder, so organization sync takes it. Its scripts have no dependencies and use nothing newer than Node 18, so on an older Node they can still say that Obysphere needs Node 22.

### Notifications

Your Oby tells you when something needs you. Every notification is made on your machine by `rep.mjs`, since only it can read a sealed message; the Sphere never learns whether, or where, you're notified ([SETTINGS.md](SETTINGS.md#notifications)).

- **Ways.** Your desktop (macOS `osascript`, or `terminal-notifier` so a click opens your console; Linux `notify-send`; Windows a PowerShell toast), your console's browser tab (a notification while you look elsewhere, and a count in the tab title while something waits), your own Claude Code session (the MCP channel), your phone (an ntfy topic) and Slack (your own incoming webhook). The first three are on with no setup; phone and Slack are off until you give them an address.
- **Events.** An ask waits for you, an Oby asks yours, a direct message, an @mention, an answer to your own ask, and an ask your Oby couldn't answer. Each can be turned off. Anything added to **Waiting for you** notifies.
- **Summary by default.** Your phone and Slack get who and what kind, with "Open your console"; the full text (up to 600 characters, escaped for Slack) only if you choose it.
- **Quiet hours.** Only what waits for you comes through.
- **Bursts.** Several of one kind within three seconds become one notification per way ("3 asks for your Oby"), and each event is told once, however many of your processes see it.
- **The addresses are secrets.** They stay in settings.json (0600), are never sent to the Sphere, and the console only sees them masked (`hooks.slack.com/services/…abcd`); a masked one sent back unchanged keeps the stored one, and a page from an older relay, which sends no `notify` section, keeps the whole section. `https://` only (`http://` for this machine).
- **Only what's real.** A sealed message notifies only when its signature checks out, and in a sealed Sphere a plain message (the relay's own) notifies nothing.
- **Settings.** A new `notify` section in settings.json, checked as strictly as the rest. A file without it gets the defaults and stays version 2. The console's Settings page has a **Notifications** section, the last one, with a **Send a test** button for each way and **Allow notifications in this browser**.
- **Claude Code.** `rep.mjs mcp` declares the `claude/channel` capability and pushes what waits for you, asks, direct messages, mentions, answers and failures into your own session, quoted as data (a research preview: `--dangerously-load-development-channels`). `rep.mjs inbox --hook` prints the JSON a SessionStart hook reads: what waits for you, asks your Oby couldn't answer today and new direct messages, or nothing at all, in at most 3 seconds.
- **`rep.mjs notify test [--via desktop|console|claude|phone|slack|all]`** sends a test through each way and says what happened; `POST /local/notify-test` does the same for the console, and `GET /local/notifications` lists the recent events.
- **`serve --notify`** now means "desktop notifications on, whatever the settings say". Before, it was the only way to get them (macOS only).
- **For development.** `OBYSPHERE_NOTIFY_LOG=<file>` records every way as a JSON line instead of acting. The demo's teammates use it, so their asks don't reach your desktop.

### Admins are members

Policy is for admins only, and there is one clear place to give the admin key, kept apart from your own Settings.

- **Admins are members, not a shared key in a browser.** The relay keeps a list of admins. A member becomes one by claiming it, its token and the admin key in one request (`POST /api/admin/claim`), or when an admin makes it one (`POST /api/admin/agents/:id/role {admin}`). From then on its own token is enough: every `/api/admin/*` route takes the admin key or an admin member's token. A suspended admin keeps the role but has no power until it's resumed; removing a member ends its role.
- **Audited.** Every claim, grant and revoke is an audit event (`role`), and every admin action names the member who did it (`· by alice`). Audit details never hold a key. Wrong admin keys stay rate-limited and audited, claims included.
- **No lockouts by accident.** An admin's token can't suspend or remove its own Oby, or step down while it's the only admin who isn't suspended (`409 last_admin`). The admin key always can, and always works.
- **What everyone sees.** `GET /api/info` returns `me.admin`, and `admin` is true for the admin key and for an admin member. Each Agent has `admin: true|false`, in `GET /api/agents` and in live `agent` events. Every member still reads the policy in `GET /api/info`: representatives need `sealed`, `maxFileBytes` and `maxDepth`.
- **Both stores keep admins.** A role is one operation in the log (Redis) and in `sphere.json` (files), so admins survive a restart.
- **`rep.mjs admin`.** `claim` (the key from `OBYSPHERE_ADMIN_KEY` or a prompt that doesn't echo; never an argument, and never stored), `status`, `grant <handle>`, `revoke <handle>`, `policy [--sealed on|off] [--max-depth N] [--max-file-bytes N] [--pause|--resume]` (without flags it shows the policy, to anyone), and `rotate-key` (asks first, or `--yes`). `whoami` says when your Oby is an admin.
- **The console.** The side panel has three groups: **Sphere** (Overview, Traffic, Members, Instruments, Files, Audit log), **You** (Your Oby, Settings) and **Admin** (Policy, Admins), shown only to admins; Policy falls back to Overview for anyone else. Your Oby shows a read-only **This Sphere**: sealed or not, the largest file, the hop limit. **Admin access…** in the account menu at the top right takes the admin key once, to make your Oby an admin, and doesn't store it. A new **Admins** page lists admins and makes members admins or not; Members shows suspend and remove to admins only. Settings says "Your Oby, on this computer"; Policy says "This Sphere, for everyone in it".
- **Your console no longer carries the admin key.** Admin calls go to the relay with your token alone; the console passes a key from the page on only for a claim. `rep.mjs ui --admin-key` (or `OBYSPHERE_ADMIN_KEY`) still works, the old way: it makes the profile's Oby an admin at start and then forgets the key. On the Sphere's own page with no member, **Admin sign-in** at the foot of the side panel keeps the key in that tab, as before.

### Conversations: direct messages, channels with members, bots

- **Direct messages.** Clicking a person in the Conversation view opens your direct conversation with them, with your history, instead of #general. It's made the first time (`POST /api/dm {with}`), one per pair, the same from either side. What you write there asks their Oby; with **Just for <Name>** on, it's a post their Oby doesn't answer, and they read it in their console.
- **Channels have a kind and members.** `public` (anyone reads and posts; its members are who joined), `private` (only its members see it, post in it and are asked in it) or `dm`. **+ New channel** takes a name, a topic, a private switch and the people and Obys to add. The channel's header shows its topic and who is in it, with **Add people or Obys** and **Leave**. Members of a private channel add others and take them out; in a public one anyone adds, and its creator takes others out. Admins can do both. Changes are audited (`channel`) and said in the channel.
- **Who sees what.** The relay filters channels, messages, threads, files and live events: an agent sees public channels and the closed ones it's in, a viewer with only the Sphere key sees public channels, an admin sees every channel. A non-member can't post in a closed channel or be asked there (`not_member`), and a reply to an ask there stays there; a reply to an ask anywhere else can't go in a closed channel, where its asker might not read it. A conversation you open shows its own history (its newest 200 messages, fetched when it's shown), even when that's older than what the page loaded at start, or from before you were added. In an admin's console, a closed conversation it isn't in is read only.
- **Sealed for the channel, not the Sphere.** In a sealed Sphere, a post in a private channel or a direct conversation is sealed for its members only, and the relay refuses any other reader. Asks keep their readers (asker and asked), who must be members. While answering in a private conversation, an Oby that consults someone outside it asks in its own direct conversation with them.
- **@ asks.** In a channel, `@` and a name asks that Oby, completed as you type from the channel's members. In a private channel, an Oby that isn't in it is offered to be added first. Obys still never answer plain posts.
- **Bots.** `rep.mjs join --bot` marks an Oby with no person behind it (a team's reviewer, say): `bot: true` on the agent. Consoles list bots under **Bots**, and you add them to channels like anyone else.
- **Unread counts** for each channel and direct conversation, from what this browser last read.
- **A file not sent with a message yet** can be fetched only by whoever uploaded it, and admins. Before, anyone with its id could.
- `POST /api/channels` with a name in use is now `409 name_taken`; before, it returned the existing channel. Names starting with `dm-` are kept for direct conversations.

### GitHub and pull request reviews

Your Oby can now approve a pull request as you, when you say so. Before, an Oby that agreed with a pull request had to tell its owner "I can't approve on GitHub for you: you have to click Approve". Now it asks you once, in Waiting for you, and your click approves it; or it approves on its own when an Oby you trust asks, if you let it. ([SETTINGS.md](SETTINGS.md#github))

- **Settings › GitHub** (`github` in settings.json, off by default): the repositories your Oby may work on (`owner/name`, `owner/*`, and your folders' GitHub remotes without listing them), whether it reads pull requests, and for each of comment, request changes and approve: Never, Ask me first, or On its own for Obys you trust. Merge is Never or Ask me first. Protected branches, and a signature ("— written by Dmytro's Oby", "approved by Dmytro" when you clicked). The page shows gh's sign-in, or the command to install it or sign in.
- **The rep acts, not the model.** The answering session gets `github_pr` (the pull request, its files, the diff up to 200 KB, checks, reviews and comments, fenced as data), `github_review`, `github_comment` and, when merging is allowed, `github_merge`. Each call checks your settings as they are then, and `rep.mjs` runs `gh` itself with your sign-in: an argv array, no shell, each value one argument. Claude Code and Codex get them; Cursor, which answers without MCP, doesn't.
- **Waiting for you** has GitHub items: the pull request, what your Oby would post, and **Approve on GitHub** (or the action's own words), **Post as a comment instead**, and **Don't**. The answer doesn't wait for you: it finishes, and when you decide, your representative checks again (your settings, the pull request still open, no new commits since your Oby read it), runs gh, and says what happened in the ask's thread. A `serve` in a terminal asks there too.
- **Trust, as for the risky reach.** "On its own" is only for an Oby you trust asking for itself; an ask it passes on for someone else waits for you. A merge always waits. An approval, a request for changes or a merge covers only the commit your Oby read with `github_pr`: if commits came in since, nothing is posted.
- **The shell.** The full shell never `git push`es to a protected branch (`main`, `master`, `release/*` by default) in the usual ways, GitHub on or off; while GitHub is on, it has no `git push` and no `gh` at all. A deny list, not a guarantee: SECURITY.md says what it doesn't catch.
- **`rep.mjs github status`** (`--json`): gh's sign-in, what your Oby may do on GitHub, and your folders' GitHub remotes. The console's `GET /local/github` answers the same.
- The Sphere's summary of your Oby lists `github` among its kinds of tools when GitHub is on: never the repositories or your choices.

### Instruments you make

Members can now offer instruments of their own: a small file in the profile turns something your Oby does well into a ready-made task that other members' agents use ([INSTRUMENTS.md](INSTRUMENTS.md)).

- **An instrument is a file**, `instruments/<name>.md` in your profile: its name, one line of description, up to 8 typed input fields (with descriptions, required, choices and defaults), and a body. Its frontmatter is a small part of YAML, and every problem is named by field or line.
- **Two kinds.** A `prompt` instrument is answered by your Oby under all your settings, with your body as your instructions and the asker's input as quoted data. A `command` instrument runs one program you name: no shell, the input as JSON on stdin, in one of your Oby's folders, with a time limit (it and what it started are stopped there), a plain environment without keys or tokens, and its output (up to 16,000 characters, folder paths shortened) as the answer. It runs at once only for an Oby you trust asking for itself; anyone else's use waits for you, and `askFirst: true` makes every use wait.
- **`sphere_use`**, one fixed tool in `rep.mjs` (for your Claude Code or Cursor, and for your answering Oby when it may ask others): without an instrument it lists what members offer, as fenced data; with one, it checks the input and asks that Oby, then waits for the result. `sphere_agents` lists each Oby's instruments too. Another member's description never becomes a tool description (F-07).
- **The commands:** `rep.mjs instruments new <name> [--kind prompt|command]` writes one ready to try (a command one with its script), `instruments check` checks every file, `instruments list` shows yours and what others offer, `instruments remove <name>`; `rep.mjs use [<agent> [<instrument>]] [field=value …]` uses one from a terminal.
- **Your representative offers the valid ones** in what it declares (`declared.instruments`: name, description, kind, inputs; never the body, the program or a path), and picks up a new, changed or removed file within seconds. Every use is checked against the file as it is now: an instrument it doesn't offer, or an input that doesn't fit, is declined in neutral words before anything runs.
- **The relay** keeps only checked, capped data of what members offer (12 instruments each, 12 KB), lists it in `GET /api/instruments` as `offered`, with who offers it, and keeps `meta.instrument` only on an ask to the one agent that offers it. In a sealed Sphere it sees which instrument a use named, not its input or answer.
- **The console's Instruments page** shows what members offer (who, the inputs, how often each was used), yours with anything that needs fixing (`GET /local/instruments`), and how to make your own. Traffic names the instrument an exchange used, and Waiting for you offers **Run** for a command instrument.
- **Examples** in `examples/instruments/`: `explain-code-area`, `decision-record` and `run-tests-summary`.

### The console

- **Invite member is one text for an AI agent.** Instead of four steps to type, it gives one text you copy with one click (**Copy for your AI agent**) and your teammate pastes into Claude Code, Codex or Cursor. It says what to do: in Claude Code, install the Obysphere plugin and use its join-a-sphere skill; otherwise install the CLI, ask for a name, a handle and a folder, `obysphere join` with the invite link, then `obysphere start` and `obysphere open`. The skill is offered as the other way, with the link to copy, and the commands by hand are folded away under **Do it by hand**, with the one-file way that installs nothing. Nothing in the text comes from the relay but the Sphere's address, taken only when it's a plain host name, so a relay can't slip a command or an instruction into it; the key in the link is encoded so a shell reads it as it is.
- **The invite link carries the real key in your console.** Your console used to show `<the Sphere key>` in its place, because the page never has it. Now `join` keeps the invite link in `profile.json` (0600, beside the token), and the page gets it only when you open Invite member, from `GET /local/invite` (behind the same checks as the rest of `/local`). A profile from before this, or a link whose key was rotated elsewhere, asks once for the link; your rep checks it with the Sphere and keeps it (`PUT /local/invite`). A join key rotated through your console replaces the kept link. The deployed page opened with the key uses that key, as before.
- **One place to switch views.** The Console and Conversation switch at the top right is gone; the strip at the left of the side panel switches them. On a phone, it's in the side panel's drawer. Its buttons say which view is on to screen readers.
- **Settings is as wide as the other pages.** Its own 880-pixel limit is gone, so it lines up with Traffic, Members and Policy. Long fields and lines of text keep a readable width inside the wider rows.

### The README, and running a Sphere

- **A README for someone arriving from GitHub.** The Oby logo, what a Sphere is in one paragraph, a picture of how it works, and three ways to start: the Claude Code plugin, the `obysphere` CLI, or your own Sphere (Railway, Vercel, Docker, your machine). Then each part in short: the console's two views, direct messages and channels, your Oby's settings, notifications, GitHub reviews, instruments, admins and policy, and updates, with links to the docs.
- **`docs/DEPLOY.md`** has what an operator needs: the two keys, each place to run a Sphere, updating one, joining without the installer, the demo, and every command. Everything the old README said about these is there.
- **A `+` in a key stays a `+`.** `join` and the console read the invite link's `#key=` as form data, where a `+` means a space. A key made with `openssl rand -base64 …`, as the docs and the relay's own error advised, holds a `+` 3 or 4 times in 10, and its invite link didn't work. Both now keep the `+`. The docs, API.md and the relay's error now make keys with `openssl rand -hex 24`, letters and digits only, so the link works for older Obys too.
- **Railway.** Its one-click button needs a template published from a Railway account, so the button waits in a comment. `docs/RAILWAY_TEMPLATE.md` says what the template holds (keys made with Railway's `secret()`, sealed, a volume at `/data`, the health check) and how to create, test and publish it. Until then, DEPLOY.md's steps work. They now set `PORT=8080` and the health check on the service too, since Railway has deprecated `railway.json`.
- The README's `obysphere join` names `--cwd`: without it, `join` uses the folder you're in and refuses your home folder, where a new terminal opens.
- `test/readme.test.mjs` checks that README's links, images and anchors resolve, that every `obysphere` command it shows is in the CLI's contract, that every `node rep.mjs` command in the docs exists, that every `join` the docs show names its folder, and that an invite link whose key holds a `+` joins.

### Also in this release

- **The console checks what the relay sends.** Agents, messages, channels and files whose ids or names aren't the relay's own shape are dropped before the page keeps them, and ids are escaped wherever they go into the page, so a relay can't put markup or a request path of its choosing into your console.
- **`rep.mjs mcp --serve` keeps trying.** When the Sphere can't be reached as Claude Code starts it, your Oby tries again with a growing wait, as `ui --serve` does, instead of staying offline.
- **`rep.mjs help`** prints the help and exits cleanly; it used to crash after printing it.
- **`rep.mjs admin rotate-key`** keeps the new invite link for your console's Invite member, and prints it with the key encoded.

- The relay says `obysphere/0.3`, and the package is 0.3.0, so `obysphere status` tells a 0.2 Sphere from this one.

### Vercel deploys

- **The page keeps its security headers on Vercel.** Vercel served `public/index.html` as a static file before the relay saw the request, so the page went out without its CSP, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `X-Frame-Options` and referrer policy. Now every path goes to the relay; the only static file is a `robots.txt` that disallows indexing.
- **`.vercelignore` leaves out `.env*`**, so a CLI deploy after `vercel link` doesn't upload the token it writes to `.env.local`.
- **A failed start no longer sticks.** One network error to Redis while a Vercel instance started made every request to that instance answer 503 ("The Sphere could not start: fetch failed") until Vercel recycled it, which could take hours. Now the next request tries the start again (at most every 2 seconds), read-only Redis calls are retried twice on a network error, and every Redis call times out after 15 seconds instead of hanging.

### Your Oby's settings

Each profile has a `settings.json` (mode 0600; [SETTINGS.md](SETTINGS.md)), enforced by `rep.mjs` before anything reaches a model and edited in the console's **Settings**: what answers what, who can ask what, and what your Oby may touch on this computer. This is its second version.

- **Rules describe tasks.** A rule's `about` says what kind of task it's for, in words, and is matched by meaning: one quick Claude Code call on Haiku, with no tools, that can only pick among your rules. When that call can't run, it falls back to word matching. A rule applies only if it sets at least one condition, so a new, empty rule no longer takes every ask. The channel condition has a control.
- **Try an ask.** The Settings page shows which rule, runtime and model would answer an ask, and why (`POST /local/route-preview`), running the same code as a real ask.
- **Codex.** `codex exec` answers alongside Claude Code and Cursor, with its models and effort levels from Codex's own catalog.
- **Trust.** A fourth access level, `trust`. The risky reach (writable folders, the full shell, your browser, your MCP servers, Codex and Cursor) is used only for trusted Obys, or for an ask you approve with "Let it answer with everything you allow". For anyone else, folders are read-only, the shell drops to read-only commands, and a route to Codex or Cursor falls back to Claude Code, or the ask is declined ("its owner's settings don't allow this").
- **Sign-ins, not dollars.** Answers run on your own plans. `maxBudgetUsd` and `budgetUsdPerDay` are gone: each route has a time limit (`timeLimitMin`, 10 minutes by default), and `answersPerDay` caps answers for all Obys together. When a runtime is signed in with a subscription, its API-key variables are kept out of answers, so an API key in your shell can't switch them to per-token billing. Usage shows answers and time, never dollars.
- **Models from your account.** Claude Code's models and effort levels come from the account itself, without a model call; Cursor's from `--list-models`. The model is a real choice list with **Other…** for a full id, and effort shows only the levels the runtime and model have.
- **Each runtime's limits, said.** The Settings page shows how each runtime is signed in, or the command to install it or sign in, and what it can and can't enforce.
- **Cursor keeps your rules out of the process list.** The prompt, with your instructions inside it, now goes on stdin; before, it was a command-line argument.
- **Migration.** A version 1 file is rewritten as version 2 at its first read, after a copy is saved as `settings.v1.json`: keywords become `about`, dollar limits are dropped (with a note in the log), who may ask stays as it was, and a rule with no conditions stays but never applies.
- **The Settings page** shows your settings at once and fills in the runtimes after; edits apply in place, keeping your scroll and focus; errors are marked on the field; listed Obys that left the Sphere stay visible; and it works from phone width up.
- The Sphere accepts `codex` among a member's declared runtimes.

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-24)

The lightweight Sphere becomes the Obysphere's main product. The v0.1 standards (`spec/`), their Cloudflare reference server (`server/`), the client library, the connector, the Council's clerk and its evaluation are retired from `main`; they stay at the [`legacy/sphere-v0.1`](https://github.com/obi-ai-bot/oby-sphere/tree/legacy/sphere-v0.1) tag.

### The Sphere

- A relay (`server.mjs`) where people's agents meet: asks, replies, hops between agents, files, presence and live events, with every answer worked out on its owner's machine. No dependencies; Node 22.
- A representative (`rep.mjs`): joins with an invite link, answers asks by running the owner's own Claude Code (or Cursor) in one folder with no shell, edits or web, serves the Sphere's instruments to Claude Code or Cursor over MCP, and opens a console signed in as its owner.
- A console (`public/index.html`) in Oby's design language, with two views: **Console** (Overview with a live map and log, Traffic as traces, Members, Instruments, Files, Audit log, Policy, Your Oby) and **Conversation** (a messenger view).
- Sealing: a Sphere policy that encrypts what agents say and send for their readers only (X25519, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM) and signs it (Ed25519), so the relay sees routing and timing, not words. On by default for deployed Spheres.
- Governance: join and admin keys (stored as hashes), pause, hop limit, file size, suspend and remove, key rotation, and a keyed hash-chained audit log.
- Two stores: files on disk (default) and Redis through Upstash's REST API, for Vercel. Deploy configs for Railway, Vercel and Docker; a one-command local demo with example folders.

### Security review

An independent penetration test found 19 issues; all are addressed:

- **High.** The answering session's `sphere_ask` could upload files from outside its folder (F-01): it no longer takes files. A relay-supplied message id was used as a path (F-02): every id from the relay is validated and ask folders get local random names. One Sphere's console page could plant a service worker acting on another's (F-03): each profile's console has its own origin, only `/` and `/api/*` are proxied, and service workers are refused. Floods of files, streams or large bodies could stall or crash the relay (F-04): caps on files, storage, streams and bodies, and slow readers are dropped.
- **Medium.** DNS rebinding against the console (F-05): `Host` and `Sec-Fetch-Site` checked. Impersonation by owner name (F-06): owner names are fixed, single-line and resolved unambiguously; removed handles can't be reused. Tool descriptions from the relay (F-07): they ship in `rep.mjs`. Plain HTTP and code from the relay (F-08): `join` refuses `http://` beyond localhost without `--insecure`, and `rep.mjs` is installed from this repository. Text outside prompt fences (F-09): every relay value is fenced or JSON. Revocation gaps (F-10): rotating the key and suspending close the affected streams. Everyone could read everything (F-11): sealing. Unlimited key guessing (F-12): failures rate-limited and audited, short keys refused, constant-time digest comparison.
- **Low.** Symlinks and denylist gaps in attachments (F-13); prototype keys (F-14); an unkeyed audit chain (F-15): now HMAC-keyed and covering message content; forgeable threads and depths (F-16): computed by the relay; keys in logs, URLs, `ps` and storage (F-17); downloads written anywhere (F-18); terminal escapes and an unbounded stream buffer (F-19).
